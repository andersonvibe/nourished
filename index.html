How to save a recipe from each app
  • Quickest: in Instagram, TikTok, YouTube or Chrome tap Share, then Nourished. The recipe is read straight away. (After this update it can take a day for Nourished to appear in the share list.)
  • Instagram: tap the paper plane, then Copy link, and paste it above. Instagram often hides captions from apps, so also copy the caption (tap it, then press and hold) into the text box.
  • TikTok: tap Share, then Copy link. If the recipe is only on screen or in the comments, add screenshots.
  • YouTube: tap Share, then Copy link. Recipes are usually in the description, which the app reads.
  • Facebook: copy the link and the post's text. Facebook usually needs you to be logged in, so the text matters.
  • WhatsApp: for a forwarded video, add it above (it's in your gallery under WhatsApp Video). For a link, copy and paste it.
  • Websites: paste the link. A few big sites (such as BBC Good Food) block apps, so copy the recipe text instead.
How the food balance works

Foods are grouped by how many calories they pack into each gram. The lighter the food, the more you can eat for the same calories.

Light, under 1 kcal per gram: vegetables, fruit, broth soups, fat-free yoghurt, skyr, cottage cheese. Fill up on these. Aim for at least 30% of your calories.

Moderate, 1 to 2.4 kcal per gram: eggs, beans, lentils, tofu, wholegrain rice and pasta, potatoes, lean meat and fish. The backbone of most meals.

Dense, over 2.4 kcal per gram: cheese, nuts, seeds, oils, butter, bread, crisps, chocolate. Healthy ones are fine in measured portions. Keep these to about a quarter of your calories.

Eating for a healthier liver and heart

General guidance from the NHS and liver charities. It isn't a treatment plan; follow what your GP or dietitian tells you.

  • Fill half the plate with vegetables or salad, and aim for at least 5 portions of fruit and veg a day.
  • Swap white bread, pasta and rice for wholegrain versions, and add beans, lentils and chickpeas.
  • Choose unsaturated fats such as olive oil, nuts and seeds over butter, cheese and fatty meat.
  • Oats, beans and lentils contain soluble fibre, which helps lower cholesterol.
  • Cut back on sugary drinks, sweets, cakes and biscuits. Check the sugars line on labels.
  • Gradual weight loss of 7–10% of body weight is linked with less liver fat. Don't crash diet.
  • Keep alcohol low, and stay active most days.
